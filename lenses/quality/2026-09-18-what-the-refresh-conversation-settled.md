# What the refresh conversation settled, and what it deliberately did not

**Derived.** 2026-09-18. **A record of a conversation, not a decision by this lens.** Sean was
talking to this session believing it was the code lane; what he decided is his, and it is written
here so the code lane is not reconstructing it from a transcript. **Nothing here is binding, and
nothing here has been built** - this lens writes no code and no prototype data.

## What Sean decided

**The trait's name is `moving`, not `moves-remaining`.** He rejected `moves-remaining`, and the
existing vocabulary already has the word: `spec/data/traits.4x` declares
`{trait name:moving admits:number kept:thing}`, and `spec/console.md:69-71` writes the description
form outright - *No trait of the thing may be left out* - `{citizen defending:1} -> 8` and
`{citizen defending:0} -> 6`, never `{citizen} -> 14`. **His proposal was the spec's own notation.**

**An allowance is a key column on the thing, and things group by it.** Sean, 2026-09-18: *I want to
make sure that we don't have one scout allowed to move 3 times because there are 3 scouts with one
move each present, or allow a scout that has moved on a previous turn to pick up a move from a
scout on the place it moved to.* So:

```
{scout where:territory-1 moving:1} -> 2
{scout where:territory-1 moving:0} -> 3
```

**This is `spec/logistics.md` already**: *Things of a kind are grouped by their state, and a kind
has few states however many things of it there are.*

**Refreshing something already topped off is a no-op, not a refusal**, and therefore `put` is
needed. This was the one question put to him and he answered it. **His reason, in his words**:
*This is not a transformation recipe, it is creation. It is similar in nature to something
generated by time. The resources that come from the ground and sun are infinite but rate limited.
So are moves.*

**He rejected `has` as the word** for a per-kind maximum, agreeing it is the word containment
wants.

## What he deliberately deferred, and why deferring is free

**Whether a turn is a resource.** Sean: *I am not sure I am ready for treating turns as a resource
yet, it feels like trying to unify the 4 fundamental forces of nature before understanding what
they are.* **Nothing in the tree forces it either way** - `spec/data/kinds.4x` declares 23 kinds and
none is a turn; the turn appears only as when world recipes fire, and `{kind name:game}` carries
`phase`, which is a value rather than a quantity. So there is no row that would have to be unwritten
later.

**Whether allowance and capacity unify**, and therefore the word for a per-kind maximum -
`readies`, `allows`, `affords`. Sean: *There is a difference between a territory allowing for X
extractors and an extractor allowing for X operations per refresh... The ability to work resets
each turn, the amount allowed stays the same.*

**Deferring this costs nothing right now, and the reason is worth keeping**: while every maximum is
1, the maximum is a literal in the refresh rule, the way `move` already carries
`{literal id:1 clause:clause-4 column:73 value:1}`. **No maximum row is needed until a maximum is
not 1.** So the word and the unification arrive with the concept that needs them.

**One piece of evidence for that decision when it comes.** `spec/data/carries.4x` gives `deposit`
the traits `capacity`, `occupied` and `free`, and gives `extractor` only `working`. Capacity has
occupancy and an allowance has only a current value - `spec/logistics.md`: *capacity is spent and
given back*, where nothing is ever short of free work. **The prototype makes the two look more
alike than `spec/` does**, because `{limit held:extractor by:deposit}` compares two quantities and
drops occupied and free, so judging the question from the prototype is judging from a
simplification.

## Why `put`, measured rather than asserted

**Parsed from `releases/first-release.md` - 25 recipes, 92 role rows** (32 `consume`, 22 `require`,
21 `produce`, 17 `put`, and **0 `limit`**, a role declared and never used). Re-run with
`scratchpad/recipes.py`, which reads the seven-column recipe rows and groups them by recipe.

**Six recipes make something and consume nothing, and five of them are rate-limited by an
allowance they spend:**

```
create labor   require citizen   · put laboring  one less · produce labor
bear           require citizen   · put bearing   one less · produce fertility
muster         require citizen   · put defending one less · produce force (that citizen's strength)
stand          require unit      · put defending one less · produce force (that unit's strength)
work           require extractor · put working   one less · produce resource   (and consumes labor)
refresh        put <trait> at its maximum
```

**So refresh is the sixth and it restores what the other five spend.** That is *infinite but rate
limited* read off Sean's own table, and it makes the pattern five-plus-one rather than a special
case.

**`refresh` is the only recipe of the 25 that is a `put` alone** - no require, no consume, no
produce - which is why it is the right recipe to build the role on.

**One correction to the framing, which is this lens's and not his.** `put` runs in both
directions: `at its maximum` for `refresh` and `renew`, `one less` for six recipes, and
`keeps one less` for `age`, which is decay. **So `put` is assignment and the creation-ness lives in
having no consume row**, not in the role.

**And the release's definition of `put` already fits the grouping design**, which is the part worth
knowing before building it: *a put names a thing that is already there and says what is true of it
afterwards - the same thing and not a new one, so what has an identity keeps it. **A put has no
quantity, because nothing is made or taken.*** Under grouping a put moves a whole
description-group, whatever size it is, so *no quantity* is the natural form rather than a
restriction to work around. `Store::add` already merges descriptions, so putting
`{scout moving:0} -> 3` to `moving:1` joins an existing `-> 2` to make 5 with no new code.

## What this implies for the engine, stated as observations

**Verified against the tree; none of it is a decision.**

- **The grouping itself needs no engine change.** `Relation::key()`,
  `prototypes/thin-engine/src/schema.rs:109-120`, is every column but the quantity and any declared
  attribute - so a trait column joins the key by existing, and the two groups are two rows rather
  than a duplicate-key refusal.
- **`move` and `work` need no `put`** while the maximum is 1. Spending is a state transition the
  three existing roles already do: remove `{unit where:$from moving:1} -> 1`, add
  `{unit where:$to moving:0} -> 1`. The remove refuses when that group is empty, which **is**
  *moving at least 1* - so no comparison operator is needed either.
- **`put` is needed only by refresh**, and only because refresh must be a no-op when the group it
  would top off is absent.
- **`working` as a separate counted relation becomes unnecessary.** It exists because there was no
  `put`; the release writes the same fact as `put extractor working one less`. Under grouping it is
  a column of `extractor`, and `{limit held:working by:extractor}` goes with it - **that limit
  would otherwise become a second statement of a cap the maximum already gives**, which
  `spec/invariants.md` forbids.
- **A put conserves the count**, and that is the invariant separating it from a remove-and-add that
  could drop or duplicate: three scouts before, three after, only their descriptions changed.

## The one design question this lens will not answer

**In a put clause, which columns are the match and which are the assignment?** A `require` or
`remove` clause's bindings and literals together make one pattern. A put needs both - the rows to
find, and what is true of them afterwards - and nothing in the notation distinguishes them yet.

The cheapest reading is that **bindings match and literals assign**, so refresh binds `where` to
its input and writes `moving:1` as a literal. **That is a guess about the code lane's design and
not a recommendation**; it is written down so the question is visible rather than to answer it.

## The size of the change, so it is not discovered halfway

- `moving` joins the `unit` family, and `scout` and `transport` must declare it - enforced, since
  `Malformed::UnlikeShape` is *a relation belongs to a family and does not declare one of the
  family's columns*.
- **8 of the 17 tests name a scout or transport, 21 rows between them**, and every one of those
  rows gains a `moving:` field.
- `extractor` gains `working`; the `working` relation, its three columns, its reference rows, its
  `{state}` row and `{limit held:working by:extractor}` all go; **6 tests state a `{working ...}`
  row** in their `given` and stop needing to.
- `data/friendly/` is the source and `data/foundation/` is converted from it -
  `tests/directories.rs` - so both directories move together and the round trip is what says they
  agree.

## Four tests, drafted for the code lane to place

**These are drafts in a lens's directory, which is the only place this lens may write them.** They
are written in the friendly format; the foundation copies are the conversion the code lane's own
round trip produces. **Sean asked for no more than five at a time.**

The first three are units, which is where the hazard he named lives. The fourth is the extractor,
and it is in the set on purpose: `working` is a separate counted relation today, so doing both in
one pass is what shows the grouping is the general form rather than a special case for units.

```
{test name:a-scout-that-has-moved-cannot-move-again}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{territory id:2 name:territory-2}
{adjacency id:1 from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{scout where:territory-1 moving:1} -> 1

{when}
{move what:scout from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{move what:scout from:territory-2 to:territory-1}

{refused}
{scout where:territory-2 moving:0} -> 1
```

```
{test name:a-scout-arriving-does-not-lend-a-move-to-one-that-has-spent-its-own}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{territory id:2 name:territory-2}
{territory id:3 name:territory-3}
{adjacency id:1 from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{adjacency id:2 from:territory-2 to:territory-3}
{scout where:territory-1 moving:1} -> 1
{scout where:territory-2 moving:0} -> 1

{when}
{move what:scout from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{move what:scout from:territory-2 to:territory-3}

{refused}
{scout where:territory-2 moving:0} -> 2
```

```
{test name:refresh-makes-one-entry-of-a-spent-scout-and-a-fresh-one}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{scout where:territory-1 moving:1} -> 2
{scout where:territory-1 moving:0} -> 3

{when}
{refresh what:scout where:territory-1}

{then}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{scout where:territory-1 moving:1} -> 5
```

```
{test name:refresh-restores-a-spent-extractor}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{deposit where:territory-1 what:food density:6} -> 1
{extractor where:territory-1 what:food working:0} -> 1
{labor where:territory-1} -> 1

{when}
{refresh what:extractor where:territory-1}
{work where:territory-1 what:food}

{then}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{deposit where:territory-1 what:food density:6} -> 1
{extractor where:territory-1 what:food working:0} -> 1
{food where:territory-1} -> 6
```

**Two things about the drafts that are this lens's reading and may be wrong.**

**The second test's `{refused}` section is the interesting one and the one to check hardest.** It
says the second `move` is refused **and** that the world it would have left is a territory-2 with
two spent scouts - which is the hazard stated as a world rather than as a sentence. If `{refused}`
cannot carry that, the test needs a `{then}` and a separate refusal, and the code lane should say
so rather than bend the test.

**The fourth test's `{when}` fires refresh and then `work`**, so the evidence is that the extractor
works after being refreshed rather than that a row changed. **That is deliberate** -
`CLAUDE.md` says a check whose subject is behaviour reads the outcome and not the input, and a test
asserting only that `working` went from 0 to 1 would be satisfied by a rule that writes the column
and changes nothing about what the extractor can do. **The `{then}` shows `working:0` again**
because `work` spends what `refresh` restored, which is the whole loop in one file.

**And a no-op test is missing on purpose.** *Refresh of something already at its maximum changes
nothing* is the decision Sean made and would be the fifth test; it is left out because five was his
ceiling and this lens would rather he chose which four than be handed five. **If one of these is
dropped, that is the one to add.**
