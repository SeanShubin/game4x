# Notes on lenses, from the one that exists

**Derived.** Written by the quality instance on 2026-08-29. **Input to a decision, not an
observation about the tree** - and the decision is Sean's, written in
[`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md), which is the documentation lane's file. Nothing here is a finding.

[Quality](README.md) · [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)

Sean is considering a specification lane, a coding lane, and several research lanes - this being
one - and thinks **lens** may be the better word, since each takes the same problem with a different
focus. Each perspective would get a directory it may freely edit, and specification and code would
pull what they need from the others and from each other.

What follows is what three reports from inside the only lens that exists suggest about that,
including the parts that argue against it.

---

## 1. The word is right for the research perspectives and wrong for the other two

**Lens** captures something *lane* actively obscures. A lane is a track you stay in; the
metaphor's whole force is *don't cross*, which describes the writing rule and mis-describes the
reading rule. Every perspective here reads everything. They are not parallel tracks - they are one
subject viewed from different places, which is exactly what a lens is.

But it flattens a distinction that has already done work this week. **Specification and code are not
focuses on the problem. They are the artifacts.** `spec/` is the destination; `crates/` is the thing
that ships. A research lens produces *claims about* those artifacts and ships nothing.

The asymmetry has teeth in one specific way:

|                     | Producers (`spec/`, `crates/`) | Lenses (`quality/`, and any others)     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Output is           | the thing itself               | a claim about the thing                 |
| Wrong output costs  | a broken product               | a producer's attention, or a bad change |
| Can be acted on by  | nobody - it *is* the act       | only a producer                         |
| Correctness checked | by tests, by the gate, by Sean | **by a producer disagreeing**           |

That last row is not hypothetical. [Finding 1 of the last report](2026-08-29-coupling-under-the-game.md#1)
was wrong, and would have broken both an existing test and a specification rule had anyone acted on
it. The code lane refused it, I checked and withdrew it, and the exchange produced better output
than my finding did - the measurement, and a sharper framing of the underlying tension. **A lens
being refuted is the system working**, and it is a mode the producers have no equivalent of.

So I would keep two words. Lenses for the research perspectives; something else for the two that
produce. Sean's own sentence already treats them differently - *"specification and code pull from
the others and each other"* - and the vocabulary should carry that rather than fight it.

## 2. What breaks at N lenses is attention, and `CLAUDE.md` already knows the number

The scarce resource is not disk or naming. It is how many claims two producers can read, judge and
act on before reading costs more than doing. `CLAUDE.md` already states the threshold for the one
queue that exists:

> Keep the open-proposal queue under fifteen. Past that, reviewing costs as much as writing and the
> mechanism has failed.

Five lenses each holding themselves to a sane number is not five sane numbers. It is seventy-five,
which is a failure by the repository's own stated limit. And a lens has no way to see the total: it
knows its own findings and nothing about what the other four filed this morning.

**So the budget belongs on the aggregate, not per lens.** That is the single most important
consequence of going from one lens to several, and it does not follow automatically from giving each
one a directory.

It also implies something uncomfortable and worth saying plainly: **if the aggregate is capped, a
lens producing many true findings crowds out another lens's fewer, better ones.** Whatever the rule
becomes, it should make a lens compete on the value of a finding rather than on the count.

---

## 3. What has to change *before* a second lens, not after

The current report format is prose in a padded markdown table. It works because there is one lens
and I can hand-maintain it. It will not survive five, and the evidence is in this repository rather
than in principle.

Hand-maintained cross-references here have rotted every time they have been tried:

- Thirteen rows went missing from `proposals.md`.
- Three files were dropped from the specification index.
- The quotation sweep found eight of eleven, twice, before a test was written.
- Marking report 1's findings closed meant rebuilding a table row by prefix, because the padder had
  repadded the bytes my match string was written against.

The pattern that *has* worked, every time, is making agreement checkable by a program:
`command_language::agreement::disagreements`, `quotations.rs`, `what_is_carried_is_what_is_on_disk`,
and `first_release.rs` parsing the release's own tables. `tools/pad-tables` is precedent for a
documentation tool outside the workspace.

**So: a finding needs a stable identity and a machine-readable status before there are five lenses
producing them.** Something a tool can walk - a heading convention, or front matter - carrying at
minimum an id, the column that owns it, and whether it is open, acted on, rejected or withdrawn.
Then the index is generated rather than maintained, and "how many findings are open across every
lens" is a command rather than a careful read of five directories.

Doing this with one lens costs an afternoon and one migration. Doing it with five costs five
migrations and a period where the answer is unknown.

## 4. Pull is right for content and wrong for the guarantee

*Pull* is the correct model for what a producer acts on. The producer knows its own budget and its
own priorities; a lens does not, and a lens that could push work into `crates/` would be a second
author of the code with none of the responsibility. Report 3's findings 4, 5 and 6 were pulled and
fixed; findings 2, 3 and 7 were left, correctly, because the code lane judged the cost. That is the
model working.

But pull cannot carry a *guarantee*, because nobody pulls what they do not know exists. That is
precisely what happened last week: P-123's contradiction sat in `quality/` for a day while
`proposals.md` was empty, and the empty queue means no contradictions promise was false the whole
time. The fix just landed - *"Read the quality reports before claiming the queue is clean"* - is a
push rule wearing a checklist, and it works because there is one directory to read.

At five it becomes "read five directories before claiming anything," which is a checklist, and
checklists rot the same way indexes do. **Pull the content; generate the guarantee.** The tool from
§3 is what makes "nothing known is outstanding anywhere" a fact rather than a habit.

---

## 5. Refutation needs a resting place, and currently has none

When the code lane refused finding 1, it was right, and the only reason the correction exists is
that Sean relayed the objection to me. Had he not, my report would still say **Fix now** on a change
that breaks `two_regions_meeting_at_a_point_agree_about_it` and a `spec/planet.md` rule - and it
would have said so to whoever read it next.

That is survivable with one lens and an attentive Sean in the loop. It is not survivable with five,
because the whole point of several lenses is that Sean is not personally routing every claim.

A producer cannot write into a lens's directory - that is the rule that makes lenses trustworthy -
so the rejection has to land somewhere the lens's index can see it. The cheapest place already
exists: **the commit that declines it.** This repository's commit messages are unusually rich and
already cite findings by number - `b43d9b4` names findings 5, 6 and 4 and says what it did with
each, and says of finding 1 that it is *"real and is not fixed here."* Making that a convention a
tool can parse costs close to nothing and closes the loop mechanically:

> `finding: report-3/4 acted` · `finding: report-3/1 rejected - conflicts with spec/planet.md`

The lens then reconciles against what the producers actually did, rather than against what it
assumed they would.

## 6. Directory shape, and what moving costs today

`quality/` at the root does not generalise. Five sibling directories at the root would put research
output at the same level as `spec/`, `crates/` and `docs/`, which reads as more authority than a
lens has, and gives a tool no single glob.

`lenses/quality/`, `lenses/performance/`, and so on gives one place to sweep, one glob, and a name
that says what the contents are for.

The cost of moving is six references, which I counted rather than estimated: three in `CLAUDE.md`,
three in `docs/notes/proposals.md`. It will only ever be more expensive than it is today.

---

## 7. The test a proposed lens should have to pass

This repository already has the right test, for crates:

> A crate earns its existence by having a boundary someone else could reasonably want to cross.
> "This file got long" is not a boundary.

The analogue: **a lens earns its existence if it produces findings neither producer would produce.**
That is exactly how `quality/README.md` justifies this one - *"neither of the other two lanes is
looking for it: the code instance reads the spec as instructions and the documentation instance does
not read the code."*

Applying it honestly to my own three reports, because it is the only calibration data available:

| Finding                                                                                                                        | Would a producer have found it?                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Spec-versus-code contradictions - reset unreachable on touch, the digit keys named nowhere, misquoted rules, the biome tension | **No.** This is where the lens earned its existence                |
| The routing gap, and the queue's broken promise                                                                                | **No.** Neither producer can see the whole protocol from inside it |
| `planet-terrain` missing from the gate lists                                                                                   | Probably - it is a checklist the code lane owns                    |
| The detached globe's readout advertising absent keys                                                                           | Probably, on next running the prototype                            |
| Palette duplication, dead code, two option parsers                                                                             | Yes, eventually, from a careful read                               |

Roughly a third of what I filed met the test, and that third is the whole justification. **A second
lens should have to say, before it exists, what class of finding it will produce that neither
producer would** - and if the honest answer is "the same things, sooner," that is a case for a
better-instructed code lane rather than a new directory.

---

## What I would do, in order

Not a decision - Sean's, and `CLAUDE.md`'s to write. This is what the evidence above points at:

1. **Keep the two words.** Lenses for the research perspectives; the producers are something else.
2. **Give findings a machine-readable identity and status, while there is one lens to migrate.**
3. **Move to `lenses/quality/` in the same change**, at a cost of six references.
4. **Write the index generator** - it is what turns *pull the content* into a guarantee, and it is
   the same shape as four things this repository already does well.
5. **Put the budget on the aggregate**, and make the generator print it.
6. **Only then add a second lens**, and make it answer §7 first.

The ordering matters more than any individual item. Every mechanism in this repository that works
was built before the thing it guards got large - and the two that were retrofitted, the proposals
queue and the quotation guard, each lost material first.
