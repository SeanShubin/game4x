A pioneer standing on the ground, and the settlement it becomes standing where it stood.

Sean, 2026-09-20: Pioneer [...] can deploy with the same result as an ark, and *pioneer
deploys from same territory, surface deploys to surface.*

Both of those sentences are consequences here rather than rules. deploy asks for the place
it is given and then for the surface of that place's territory - for an ark in orbit those are two
places, and for a pioneer already on the surface they are one place, matched twice. Nothing in
the rule distinguishes the cases and nothing needs to.

What made one rule serve both was taking a literal away. deploy used to require the place it
was given to be an orbit. It no longer says, because {stands-in ...} already does: an ark may
only be in an orbit and a pioneer only on a surface, so what each can be asked to do was settled
before the rule was asked.

The founder is an argument, which is what keeps this from being a second rule. Sean, twice:
*we are never taking a solution of the 'metal-bin, food-bin, metal-transport' shape, we are always
keeping parameterization.* A third kind that founds is a {member ...} row.

This is the piece the win condition needs. Launching from a space other than the one the ark
came down on takes a second settlement, and nothing else in the game can make one: a citizen is
not a unit, so move cannot carry one.
