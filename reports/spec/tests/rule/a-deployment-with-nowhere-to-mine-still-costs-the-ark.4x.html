An ark deployed onto ground with nothing to mine: the ark is spent, the citizens land, and
neither extractor is placed.

Sean, 2026-09-20: *it is legal to deploy an ark anywhere, individual rules may fail but the
deployment succeeds. Consequences of legal moves may be disastrous, but that is player choice.*
This is the disaster, and the engine lets the player have it.

The ark is removed before anything is made, which is the clause order, so a deployment that
places no extractor still costs an ark. That is the whole reason the two lines are soft: a
rule refuses the world it would leave, and refusing here would have meant a deployment onto
barren ground could not happen at all.

The capacity row is what makes this a refusal to place rather than a free pass. With no
capacity for extractors stated, nothing bounds them and both would land - *nothing says how much
room there is and there is no room* are different worlds. This test states the first so that it
can be the second.

The citizens are not soft and land anyway. Nothing gives room for a citizen, so a citizen can
never be short - and it is the absence of a capacity row that says so rather than anything in the
rule.
