A bin holds ten metal and a transport holds ten, and twenty metal fits in the two of them.

A place's room is the sum of what stands in it, which is spec/logistics.md's sentence:
a place's capacity for a kind is the sum of what is in it that can hold that kind. The first
version of the check compared each capacity row on its own and refused this world, and no test
caught it because none had two kinds of container for one resource.

Sean, 2026-09-19, on what a transport does, which is what found it: *transport would not
automatically take what they haul, they would provide the capacity necessary to move the resource
they haul.* So a transport is a bin that moves - it gives room where it stands and holds nothing,
and nothing has to track which container a resource is in.

Exactly full is the point. Twenty in twenty passes only if the two are added; under the
version that compared one row at a time, twenty against ten was refused. Deleting either
capacity row makes this red, which is what says the sum is being tested rather than mentioned.

The metal is the territory's and not the transport's. When the transport leaves, the room
leaves with it and the metal stays - spec/logistics.md: *nothing falls loose, because nothing
was inside it*. What is then over the line is lost at the turn's end, which is the step the turn
does not have yet.
