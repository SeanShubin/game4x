The same free space admits one kind and refuses another.

Sean, 2026-09-18: *It is certainly valid to test that different berth sizes work properly,
but we don't specifically care what those sizes are for what units. *So the numbers are not the
subject. The rate belongs to the consumer, and two kinds meeting the same room get different
answers - which is a fact about the engine and not about how big a transport is.

Neither number is stated outside this prototype. berth, scout and transport appear
nowhere in spec/ or releases/, which is S-136's isolation working - so a name carrying 2
and 1 would read as authoritative about something nothing states, and would go on reading that
way after the numbers changed.

The other half of the argument is in a-scout-moves-where-there-is-a-berth-to-spare, which
puts a scout into this same room and succeeds. A test states a then or a refused and not both
- Failed::BothEndings - so the contrast has to live in two files, and naming the other one is
what makes the pointer followable and gives a later rename something to find.

{test name:the-same-free-space-admits-one-kind-and-refuses-another}

{given}
{provides kind:place what:berth} -> 6
{consumes kind:scout what:berth} -> 1
{consumes kind:transport what:berth} -> 2
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{territory id:2 name:territory-2}
{place id:2 of:territory-2 layer:surface name:place-2}
{adjacency id:1 from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{transport where:place-1 moving:1} -> 1
{transport where:place-2 moving:1} -> 2
{scout where:place-2 moving:1} -> 1

{when}
{move what:transport from:place-1 to:place-2}

{refused}
{provides kind:place what:berth} -> 7
