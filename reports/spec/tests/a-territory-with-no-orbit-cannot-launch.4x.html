Everything an ark costs, standing on a territory with no orbit above it, and the launch refused.

The orbit is found by matching and not by being named, so there is nothing to get wrong and
nothing to check: a territory that has no orbit has no place for the rule to put an ark, and the
pattern it asks for is not there.

This is the same shape as a scout with no border to cross. What would be a question - *is
there an orbit?* - is a clause that matches or does not, and the refusal names the row it wanted.

The resources are untouched. A rule that cannot do all of what it says does none of it, so a
refused launch costs nothing - which is the default this engine has and the reason a line that may
fall short has to say so.

{test name:a-territory-with-no-orbit-cannot-launch}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{labor where:place-1} -> 1
{metal where:place-1} -> 1
{energy where:place-1} -> 1

{when}
{launch where:place-1}

{refused}
{place of:territory-1 layer:orbit}
