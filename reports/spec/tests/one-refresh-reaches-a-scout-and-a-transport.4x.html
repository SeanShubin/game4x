One command refreshes two kinds, because unit is the family they both belong to.

Sean, 2026-09-18: *I should be able to declare separate things with separate commands, as
well as explicitly declare group commands. So i can refresh a scout, refresh a transport,
refresh all movable, refresh all workable, and their intersections.* This is the group form;
refresh-makes-one-entry-of-a-spent-scout-and-a-fresh-one is the separate one, and the two are
the same rule with a different argument.

Two kinds and not two rules is the whole point. what:unit denotes every member of the
family, so adding a third kind of unit changes nothing here - where a rule per kind would need
a fourth rule and a fifth.

Each kind starts with a spent group and a fresh one, so the two join and the count is the
sum: two scouts and one, three; one transport and two, three. The numbers are crossed between
the kinds - 2 and 1 for the scout, 1 and 2 for the transport - so a test that read one kind's
row for the other's would not add up.

Every value in the given is load-bearing, and the mutation sweep is what says so. An
earlier draft started both kinds wholly spent; changing either moving:0 to moving:1 then
made the command a no-op and the then a copy of the given, and the test passed having
refreshed nothing. A spent group beside a fresh one is what closes that - flipping either
value now puts two rows of one kind on one key, which the structure refuses.

Poisoned three ways besides. Refreshing what:scout leaves the transports spent;
refreshing what:transport leaves the scouts spent; and trait:working is refused, because no
unit carries one.

{test name:one-refresh-reaches-a-scout-and-a-transport}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{scout where:place-1 moving:0} -> 2
{scout where:place-1 moving:1} -> 1
{transport where:place-1 moving:0} -> 1
{transport where:place-1 moving:1} -> 2

{when}
{refresh what:unit trait:moving}

{then}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{scout where:place-1 moving:1} -> 3
{transport where:place-1 moving:1} -> 3
