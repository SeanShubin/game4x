One step of the turn, fired on its own: the metal over capacity goes.

A part is a rule and a test may name it. Sean, 2026-09-18: *it will be easier to test the end
turn command itself if i can test its parts.* ending-a-turn-takes-what-is-in-disorder fires the
whole turn over this same world; this fires one step of it, and the two differ only in the
command - which is the point rather than a redundancy.

What the pair buys is a diagnosis. Delete the {part ...} row and the composite test goes red
while this one stays green: the turn stopped calling it rather than the rule broke. One test
could not tell those apart.

A first draft asserted that a spent scout stayed spent, which is testing that a rule does not
do something no rule of its shape could do. Sean, 2026-09-19: *it seems to test that we don't do
something we wouldn't do anyways.* The scout is gone.

It is also what the mutation sweep asked for: with no test naming discard-disorder, the
rule's name is read by nothing, and the sweep says so.

It names nothing, because there is nothing to name. The clause names stock - the family of
everything that may lie loose - so the rule trims all of it and the turn fires it once. There is
no which: whatever is in disorder goes.

Two bins at ten apiece is twenty, and twenty-two is two over.
