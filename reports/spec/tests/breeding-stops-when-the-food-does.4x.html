Three citizens and five food become five citizens, because the food ran out before the parents
did.

The other bound, and the pair with three-citizens-and-ten-food-become-six: there, breed
stopped for want of parents; here it stops for want of food. The rule cannot tell which bound it
hit and does not need to - it fires until a clause stops matching.

Three eaten leaves two to spare, and two bred is an increase of two. That is
min(food to spare, citizens that can bear) arriving without a comparison anywhere, which is what
repeating a small rule buys over writing the quantity down.

The one citizen that did not breed is indistinguishable at the end. It ends the turn fertile
and hungry like the four that did, because the turn restores the count rather than remembering
who spent it.

{test name:breeding-stops-when-the-food-does}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{citizen where:place-1 hungry:1 bearing:1 laboring:1} -> 3
{food where:place-1} -> 5

{when}
{end-turn}

{then}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{citizen where:place-1 hungry:1 bearing:1 laboring:1} -> 5
