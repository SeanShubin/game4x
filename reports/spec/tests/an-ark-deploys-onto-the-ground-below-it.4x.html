Two territories, both with ground and both with deposits, one orbit - and the settlement lands

The index · the file itself, as text

Two territories, both with ground and both with deposits, one orbit - and the settlement lands
under the ark rather than on the other one.

where is where the ark is, not where the settlement goes. The command names the orbit,
because that is where the thing being spent is standing - the same way launch names the surface
the resources are on and puts the ark it makes in the orbit above.

The ground is found by matching and this is what says so. The rule reads the orbit's territory
and asks for the place that has it and is a surface. Take the reading away and the clause
matches both surfaces - and because a clause several rows answer remembers none of them, the
rule is refused rather than guessing. The engine has no way to pick and does not have one here
either.

The mutation sweep asked for this test. Every other deployment world has one surface place, so
the reading was masked: with nothing else to match, a clause that reads a territory and a clause
that reads none agree. Sean, 2026-09-15: *We should never have non-determinism from what row
happens to be encountered first.*

Both grounds are equally good, which is what makes the answer mean anything. Each territory
has a metal deposit and a food one, so the settlement is not on place-1 for having the
resources - it is there for being under the ark. A first draft put the deposits on the far
territory only and the deployment placed no extractors at all, which read as a failure and told
nobody anything: it excluded a rule that goes looking for good ground, and no such rule is
available to an engine that matches patterns.
