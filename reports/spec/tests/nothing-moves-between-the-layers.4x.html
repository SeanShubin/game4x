A scout on the ground, an orbit next door, and the move refused.

Sean, 2026-09-20: for now I am thinking nothing will move between layers. So the two places a
move names must share a layer, and a surface and an orbit never do.

Nothing says no. The to place takes its layer from the from place's, so the pattern asks
for a surface place with that id and there is not one - what would be an equality test is a
match that fails, which is the move this engine makes everywhere a comparison would do.

Adjacency is why this needed saying. The graph joins territories and knows nothing of layers,
so the orbit above a neighbour is exactly as adjacent as its ground - and until the layers had to
agree, a scout could walk into space.

Crossing still happens and is never a move. Deploying an ark takes it out of an orbit and puts
what it becomes on the ground; building one takes resources off the ground and puts an ark in
orbit. Both are transformations rather than journeys.

{test name:nothing-moves-between-the-layers}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{territory id:2 name:territory-2}
{place id:2 of:territory-2 layer:orbit name:place-2}
{adjacency id:1 from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{scout where:place-1 moving:1} -> 1

{when}
{move what:scout from:place-1 to:place-2}

{refused}
{place id:2 layer:surface name:place-2}
