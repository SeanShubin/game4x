A scout with one move in it, moved, and then asked to move back.

Sean, 2026-09-18, on why an allowance is a column of the thing: *I want to make sure that we
don't have one scout allowed to move 3 times because there are 3 scouts with one move each
present.* This is the same hazard with one scout and two commands.

Territory 2 touches territory 1 here, which it does in no other test - so the move back is
refused for want of a move and not for want of a border.

{test name:a-scout-that-has-moved-cannot-move-again}

{given}
{territory id:1 name:territory-1}
{place id:1 of:territory-1 layer:surface name:place-1}
{territory id:2 name:territory-2}
{place id:2 of:territory-2 layer:surface name:place-2}
{adjacency id:1 from:territory-1 to:territory-2}
{adjacency id:2 from:territory-2 to:territory-1}
{scout where:place-1 moving:1} -> 1

{when}
{move what:scout from:place-1 to:place-2}
{move what:scout from:place-2 to:place-1}

{refused}
{scout where:place-2 moving:1} -> 1
