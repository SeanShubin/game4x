## Where the arc comes from

The spine is reviewed/a-second-settlement-launches-the-ark-the-first-could-not.4x, which is
already D-5's four clauses: an ark lands, the first place is developed, a pioneer takes the
second by land, and the ark leaves from there.

## Every settled place works its food every turn

Sean, 2026-09-26: *every territory should have a food deposit with a density, so we should be
able to sustain a population without food stores.* Measured, and he is right: two citizens over
a food deposit of density six become six citizens in four turns with nothing stored and nobody
starving - upkeep sixteen times, breed four, perish never.

So {work ... what:food} is the first thing each settlement does on each of its turns, and
this scenario ends with two settlements of six alive rather than with two of nobody. An earlier
version of it spent turn four entirely on the launch and both settlements starved, which was the
scenario played badly and not a rule.

## What each turn is for

Turn one is the landing and the food. The ark gathers what the sun gives, then becomes two
extractors and two citizens. Food is worked, nothing eats yet - a citizen off an ark owes no meal -
so all of it is there for breed at the turn's end.

Turn two builds the pioneer and sends it. Food, then metal, then a pioneer built from them, and
it walks to the second territory - move, the one rule here that needs two places joined by a
border.

Turn three settles the second place. Two settlements from here, and both work food.

Turn four is metal on both and a bin on the first. The bin needs two capacity rows and the
first version of this file had one of them, so the bin held nothing and the turn's end took all
five metal. {capacity of:place for:bin ...} is how many bins may stand and
{capacity of:bin for:resource ...} is how much one holds - spec/logistics.md: *a place's
capacity for a kind is the sum of what is in it that can hold that kind, and for a kind a place
can hold, a place declares none of its own.*

Turn five is the second planet and the launch. Food on both, then metal, an energy extractor
built from it, the energy, and an ark built from the three - from the territory that was taken
rather than the one that was landed on, which is the half D-5 adds to the old R-6.

## perish does not fire here, and that is the spec rather than a gap

Fourteen of the fifteen rules fire. The one that does not is perish, because nobody starves -
which is what a sustained loop means.

spec/scenarios.md: *a mechanic that only appears in an unusual situation belongs in a
scenario of its own. Those are not built until the main scenario satisfies its reader.* Starvation
is that mechanic. D-5 also asks that every rule fire at least once while this runs, and those
two sentences cannot both hold - C-150.
